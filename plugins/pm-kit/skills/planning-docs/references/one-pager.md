# One-Pager

A short-form document for quick stakeholder alignment. Used when you need to pitch an idea,
summarize a direction, or get a quick read from leadership — without asking anyone to sit down
with a 10-page vision doc.

---

## Audience

Anyone who needs the gist in under two minutes. Leadership getting a first look, a colleague
being looped in, a client hearing about a planned enhancement. The reader will decide whether
to engage further based on this document.

---

## Structure

```
# [Project Name] — One-Pager

**Audience:** [me] / [team] / [client] / [dev]
**Date:** [date]
**Author:** [name]
**Status:** [e.g., Discovery, In Planning, In Development]

---

## The Problem
2-4 sentences. What pain exists today? Who feels it? Be concrete — name a
specific scenario or friction point, not an abstract gap.

## The Proposal
3-5 sentences. What are we building and why? Describe the solution at a
conceptual level — enough to understand the shape of it, not the details.

## Who Benefits
2-3 sentences. Who are the primary users and how does their experience change?

## Key Considerations
3-5 bullets. Things the reader should know: dependencies, open questions,
timeline implications, tradeoffs. Each bullet should be a single sentence.

## Next Steps
2-4 bullets. What happens next — what decisions need to be made, what work
has already started, what input is needed.
```

---

## Tone

Direct, clear, and concise. No warmup paragraphs, no hedging. Every sentence should carry
information. The reader should be able to scan this in 60 seconds and know whether they care.

---

## Common Mistakes

- **Too long.** If it's more than one page when rendered, it's not a one-pager. Cut ruthlessly.
  If you need more space, you probably need a vision doc instead.
- **Too abstract.** "We will enhance the platform's capabilities" tells the reader nothing.
  Name the specific thing being built and the specific problem it solves.
- **Burying the lead.** The problem and proposal should be immediately clear. Don't build up
  to the point — start with it.
- **Including implementation details.** No mention of APIs, database changes, or engineering
  approach. That's for the technical brief.
