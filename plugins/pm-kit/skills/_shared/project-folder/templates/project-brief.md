# Project brief template

*Filled by pm-kit's `project-brief` skill. Lives at the top of the board's Agendas page tab, above a divider; old agendas sit below it and are never touched. Reader: a CEO (theirs or ours) with five minutes. Everyone on the board can see it, client included, so it is written for both sides at once. Built from an initiative's shape (goal, success, scope) and the tracker roadmap (the road). Read `_me/voice.md` first.*

## Rules
- About 500 words. Fixed section order. A section with nothing real in it is left out, except the goal and the road.
- No `[TBD]` on the page. Every gap is a question to the user in chat before the draft is written.
- Written as Asana-safe HTML: `h2`, `p`, `strong`, `em`, `a`, `ul`, `ol`, `li` only. No tables, no `span`, no classes, no styles. Section headings are `h2`.
- Not on the page, because the board already has a home for them: project roles, key resources, milestones, status updates.
- Never on the page: who is behind on what, team capacity or effort split, internal working norms (who ignores cards, who routes through whom), the user's own untested areas.

---

**[Project name]**. [Next milestone in a phrase]. [Framing date, if the roadmap line has one]. Updated [YYYY-MM-DD].

## The goal
[Up to four sentences. What the project is, what it replaces or enables, what is done and what is next. From the tracker's Project Summary and roadmap line, or an Initiative / Vision doc in the folder if one exists.]

## What done looks like
- [Success condition, in the client's own terms where possible. From Key Decisions or a planning doc. Never invented.]
- [Two or three lines.]

## The road from here (as of [YYYY-MM-DD])
1. **[In motion / Up next / Waiting on N / Done].** [Roadmap step 1, as written in the tracker.] [At most one sentence of what the board shows moving.]
2. **[Status].** [Step 2.]

*Always a numbered list, same numbers as the tracker roadmap. Status comes from the board on the date shown.*

## What could slow us down
- **[What is stuck] (step N).** [Where it stands.] [What moves it, and who: "sits with Dana", "Sam is starting the technical conversation", "needs an owner".] [Card link]

*The candid section. Names are welcome, because getting unblocked is the point. Grammar is never accusatory: say where the work stands and who can move it, never "X hasn't done Y". Name our own side too when we hold the next move. Three to five lines, ranked by what holds up the earliest step.*

## Decisions we need
- **[Who decides]:** [the decision, as a question]. [Both positions or the current lean, one sentence.]

*Two to four lines. A disagreement between client stakeholders is written as a decision with both positions stated, never as a conflict.*

## In scope / out of scope
- In: [major areas]
- Out for now: [explicit exclusions]
